[Feeding]
Dogs that skip one meal but act normal can usually be monitored. If appetite loss lasts more than 24 hours or includes vomiting, weakness, diarrhea, or behavior changes, contact a veterinarian.

[Exercise]
Most dogs benefit from daily walks. Exercise needs depend on breed, size, age, and health. A common starting point is 20-30 minutes once or twice daily.

[Cats]
Cats need fresh water, clean litter boxes, routine feeding, and enrichment. Sudden changes in appetite, breathing, litter box use, or energy can be warning signs.

[Emergency Warning Signs]
Trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, collapse, seizures, severe bleeding, poisoning, or extreme weakness may be emergencies. Contact a veterinarian or emergency animal clinic immediately.

[General Care]
Pet owners should monitor appetite, water intake, bathroom habits, energy level, and behavior. Sudden major changes may require veterinary guidance.
